About NORTHLINK

NORTHLINK is a proposed regional development platform designed to help people discover opportunities, access practical livelihood information, communicate community priorities and navigate relevant services. It responds to an information and coordination challenge that must first be validated through community engagement and documentary review.

Important status statement: NORTHLINK is a proposed initiative and prototype. It is not currently represented as a registered organisation, funded programme, approved public service or confirmed partnership. Listings and relationships must be verified before being presented as confirmed.

Six connected focus areas

The proposed platform brings together complementary information and connection functions.

Verified opportunities

Employment, training, scholarships and enterprise support, showing source, eligibility, deadline and verification date.

Skills and livelihoods

Signposting to skills development, mentorship, entrepreneurship and practical livelihood resources.

Agriculture and markets

Agricultural information, market connections and relevant programmes, subject to verification and local relevance.

Community voice

Safe and transparent ways to collect community priorities and feedback, with privacy and consent safeguards.

Service navigation

Information about relevant services and organisations. Referrals only where providers and safe processes are confirmed.

Partnership coordination

A future coordination layer for local institutions, civil society, private sector and development actors.

Phased implementation approach

The project should begin with discovery and validation, then proceed only as scope, safeguards, capacity and resources become clear.

1

Discovery and validation

Stakeholder mapping, community listening, user interviews and review of relevant district and national plans.

2

3

Minimum viable platform

Improve the prototype through user testing and build only necessary functions, including moderated listings.

Review and scale decision

Assess evidence with users and stakeholders; revise, pause or expand according to capacity and resources.

Project concept proposal

The following sections preserve the proposal's main structure and intended commitments. Detailed operational plans, budget and geographic sequence remain subject to discovery and validation.

1. Executive summary

NORTHLINK is a proposed regional development platform designed to improve how people across Lango and Acholi discover opportunities, access practical livelihood information, communicate community priorities and navigate relevant services. It proposes six connected areas: verified opportunities; skills and livelihoods; agriculture and markets; community voice; service navigation; and partnership coordination. The first phase should validate needs and test a carefully scoped pilot before expansion.

2. Background and rationale

Information about employment, training, markets and support services may be spread across noticeboards, social media, informal networks and institutional channels. This can make it difficult to compare opportunities, establish whether a listing is genuine or current, understand eligibility or identify where to seek further information. The scale and nature of these barriers must be established through consultations and documentary review rather than assumed.

3. Problem statement

There is a need to investigate whether people and local organisations across Lango and Acholi face avoidable barriers in finding reliable, timely and relevant information about opportunities, livelihoods and services, and whether a coordinated digital and community-based approach could help.

4. Goal and objectives

Goal: Improve access to trusted information and strengthen connections between communities, opportunities and relevant support systems across Lango and Acholi.

  • Assess information and coordination needs with residents and stakeholders.
  • Design and test an accessible platform for verified opportunities and practical livelihood information.
  • Improve signposting to skills, agriculture, enterprise and service resources.
  • Establish a responsible process for receiving community feedback and identifying priorities.
  • Develop a transparent network of potential contributors and service partners, subject to due diligence and formal agreement.
  • Monitor use, information quality, user experience and outcomes.
5. Proposed geographic scope

The long-term design covers 19 administrative selections: Alebtong, Amolatar, Apac, Dokolo, Kole, Kwania, Lira District, Lira City, Otuke and Oyam in Lango; Agago, Amuru, Gulu District, Gulu City, Kitgum, Lamwo, Nwoya, Omoro and Pader in Acholi. Implementation will be phased.

6. Target users and beneficiaries
  • Young people, school leavers, graduates and jobseekers.
  • Farmers, producer groups and rural enterprises.
  • Micro and small business owners and entrepreneurs.
  • Women, youth groups and community-based organisations.
  • Residents seeking information about services and routes to providers.
  • Employers, training institutions, civil society organisations, local government and development actors who may contribute verified information.

Consultation should identify barriers affecting women, persons with disabilities, remote communities and people with limited internet access.

7. Proposed intervention components

The six focus areas are verified opportunities; skills and livelihoods; agriculture and markets; community voice; service navigation; and partnership coordination. All published information and any referral pathway require appropriate verification and safeguards.

8. Delivery approach and operating principles
  • Community-led design and consultation.
  • Verification before publication, including source, legitimacy, eligibility and dates.
  • Do no harm; protect personal and sensitive information.
  • Accessibility through low-bandwidth design, plain language and exploration of offline or assisted access.
  • Transparency about confirmed, proposed, user-submitted and unverified information.
  • Accountability through reporting, correction and review processes.
  • Partnership integrity: claim partner status only after formal confirmation.
9. Proposed implementation phases

Phase 1: Discovery and validation. Phase 2: Pilot design. Phase 3: Minimum viable platform. Phase 4: Controlled pilot. Phase 5: Review and scale decision. Detailed calendar and geographic sequence should follow discovery. A 6–12 month planning window may be considered for costing, but is not a confirmed commitment.

10. Governance and management

Awongo Duncan is identified as Founder and Innovation Lead for the proposed initiative. The project is currently founder-led. Governance should be developed before implementation, including oversight, finance, safeguarding, data protection, technical administration and complaints responsibilities. Technical support, community facilitators, district focal persons and an advisory or governance group are proposed roles, not confirmed appointments.

11. Monitoring, evaluation and learning

Establish a baseline during discovery and set targets only after pilot scope and baseline are known. Potential indicators include reach and accessibility; information quality; engagement and response time; user-reported usefulness; consent-based connections through confirmed providers; complaints resolved; and improvements made from evidence. Disaggregate data only where necessary, safe, proportionate and consent-supported.

12. Safeguarding, privacy and risk management
  • Reduce misinformation and fraud through source checks, verification dates, moderation and reporting.
  • Minimise personal data, use access controls and establish retention and deletion procedures.
  • Do not promise unsafe or unverified referrals.
  • Address digital exclusion through low-bandwidth, plain language and assisted access options.
  • Build trust through early engagement and transparent correction processes.
  • Expand only when evidence, resources and local arrangements support it.
  • Never imply institutional endorsement or partnership without confirmation.
13. Sustainability and future development

Sustainability depends on demonstrated usefulness, community trust, manageable costs, reliable information contributors and clear governance. Potential future options include institutional collaboration, grants, CSR, in-kind technical assistance and carefully designed service agreements; none is confirmed. Technical development should be incremental and based on tested requirements.

14. Indicative resource requirements and budget development

No costed budget is set at concept stage because pilot locations, duration, staffing, hosting, fieldwork and partner contributions have not been validated. A later budget should consider consultations, transport, facilitation, research, translation, website development, hosting, security, maintenance, verification, moderation, safeguarding, privacy, monitoring and administration.

15. Support and collaboration sought

Exploratory discussions are sought with community representatives, local governments, civil society organisations, youth and farmer groups, training institutions, employers, technology professionals and development partners. The current request is for feedback, technical advice, validation opportunities and possible collaboration. Formal partnerships and funding require further discussion and written confirmation.

16. Conclusion

NORTHLINK offers a practical concept for connecting communities in Lango and Acholi to better-organised information and potential opportunities. Its value depends on responding to local priorities, maintaining accurate and current information, and operating safely and sustainably. The next step is structured discovery and validation, followed by a limited pilot with clear responsibilities and measurable learning.
